Rubber Vulcanizate for Solid Tire Tread Layer

Development and evaluation of a rubber vulcanizate using recoverable carbon black as a sustainable filler material.

This individual final-year research project focused on developing a rubber vulcanizate for the tread layer of solid tires using recoverable carbon black (rCB) as a filler. The study investigated its potential as an alternative filler while evaluating its influence on the properties and performance of the rubber compound.

The project covered material selection, formulation development, rubber compounding, specimen preparation, laboratory testing, data collection and analysis.

Prepared samples were evaluated through material and mechanical testing, including Shore A hardness measurement, to assess the characteristics of the developed vulcanizate.

The study also considered sustainability and the potential reuse of recovered carbon black in engineering applications while developing practical experience in experimental research, materials characterization and technical documentation.

R/02

Developing a Rubber Vulcanizate for the Tread Layer of Solid Tires Using Recoverable Carbon Black as Filler

3rd International Multi-Disciplinary Desk Research Conference (DRC 2025), organized by The Library, University of Kelaniya, Sri Lanka.

This research investigates the development of a sustainable rubber vulcanizate for solid tire tread applications by utilizing Recoverable Carbon Black (RCB) obtained from waste tire pyrolysis as a partial replacement for conventional carbon black (CCB).

The study compares the physical, mechanical and environmental performance of RCB and CCB-based rubber compounds, focusing on factors such as tensile strength, filler dispersion, ash content and reinforcement capability. The research highlights challenges associated with RCB, including material heterogeneity and dispersion limitations, while exploring improvement approaches such as material modification and post-treatment techniques.

The findings demonstrate the potential of RCB as a sustainable filler material, achieving competitive mechanical performance while providing significant environmental and economic benefits, including approximately 40–50% material cost reduction and around 30% reduction in CO₂ emissions. This work supports the transition towards circular economy practices and greener tire manufacturing solutions.
